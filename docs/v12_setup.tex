\section{The game and its MDP}
Orbit Wars is a real-time-strategy game on a $100\times100$ board (\cd{BOARD_SIZE}) with a
sun of radius \cd{SUN_RADIUS}$=10$ fixed at the centre $(50,50)$. \emph{Planets} are owned by a
player ($0\ldots N{-}1$) or neutral ($-1$), hold an integer \emph{garrison} of ships, and have a
\emph{production} rate (1--5 ships/tick added while owned). Planets within
\cd{ROTATION_RADIUS_LIMIT}$=50$ of the centre \emph{orbit} the sun at a per-world angular velocity
$\omega\in[0.025,0.05]$; the rest are static. A player launches \emph{fleets} from owned planets;
a fleet travels in a straight line at a ships-dependent speed, can be absorbed by the sun, and on
reaching a planet fights for it.

\paragraph{Fleet speed and aiming.} A fleet of $n$ ships moves at
\[
  v(n) = \min\!\Big(1 + (\cd{SHIP_SPEED}-1)\big(\tfrac{\log n}{\log 1000}\big)^{1.5},\ \cd{SHIP_SPEED}\Big),
  \qquad \cd{SHIP_SPEED}=6 .
\]
Headings are computed analytically to \emph{lead} an orbiting target (16 fixed-point iterations of
the intercept solve, \cd{LEAD_TARGET}), so neither the policy nor the scripts ever learn aiming ---
they choose a destination planet and the engine produces the heading.

\paragraph{Combat (official top-vs-second resolve).} On a tick, all fleets that reach a planet are
summed \emph{per player} into arrivals $\cd{arr}\in\R^{B\times E\times N}$. Let the two largest be
$a_{(1)}\ge a_{(2)}$ with $a_{(1)}$ owned by player $w$. Survivors are $s=a_{(1)}-a_{(2)}$ (an exact
tie annihilates: $s=0$). If $w$ already owns the planet it is reinforced ($+s$); otherwise $s$ is
compared to the garrison and the planet \emph{flips} to $w$ when $s$ exceeds it. This is the exact
official rule and generalizes to any $N$.

\paragraph{Comets.} Five comet events spawn on a hidden schedule
(\cd{COMET_SPAWN_STEPS}$=(50,150,250,350,450)$). Each event places 4 point-symmetric neutral comets
that sweep an elliptical path (precomputed waypoints, \cd{COMET_OFFICIAL}), carry a random garrison,
produce 1 ship/tick once captured, and expire at the end of their path. Comets are deliberately
excluded from capture/loss reward accounting (Section~\ref{sec:reward}).

\paragraph{Winning.} A side is alive while it owns a planet or has a fleet in flight. The game ends
when one side remains, or at the horizon. In 2-player the outcome is $\mathrm{sign}(s_0-s_1)$ on
total strength $s_p=\sum\text{garrisons}+\sum\text{fleet ships}$; 4-player uses either linear
\emph{placement} or official \emph{winner-take-all} (Section~\ref{sec:episodes}).

\paragraph{MDP framing.} From the acting seat's view (always re-indexed to ego~$=0$, below) the
problem is a finite-horizon discrete-time MDP: state $=$ the full board, action $=$ a per-planet
gated allocation (Section~\ref{sec:action}), transition $=$ one \cd{env_step} tick \emph{given every
other seat's simultaneous action}, reward $=$ Section~\ref{sec:reward}. Because all seats move
simultaneously, the multi-agent game is only a stationary MDP once the opponents are fixed --- which
is exactly how each learner tames it: PPO/GRPO fix the opponents to frozen league members per
rollout, and the MCTS PoC fixes them to a deterministic script.

\section{The GPU-batched simulator (\texttt{GpuEnv})}
All of training runs inside \cd{GpuEnv} (\cd{env.py}), a fully tensorized re-implementation of the
official engine. Every quantity is a \cd{(B,*)} float32 tensor on \cd{cfg.device}; one
\cd{env.step} advances all $B$ parallel envs one tick with no host sync. The simulator is
\textbf{bit-exact to the official Kaggle engine}, including the official elliptical comets
(verified world-gen-exact and tick-exact; see the project memory \cd{gpuenv-kaggle-parity} and
\cd{worldgen.py}, which mirrors \cd{REFERENCE_orbit_wars.py} draw-order for draw-order).

\paragraph{State tensors.} Planets: \cd{p_alive}, \cd{p_owner}, \cd{p_x}, \cd{p_y}, \cd{p_radius},
\cd{p_ships}, \cd{p_prod}, \cd{p_is_comet}, \cd{p_init_x/y} (orbit anchor), \cd{p_rotates},
\cd{p_comet_vx/vy}, each \cd{(B,Ec)} with \cd{Ec=PLANET_CAP=48}. Fleets: \cd{f_alive},
\cd{f_owner}, \cd{f_x}, \cd{f_y}, \cd{f_angle}, \cd{f_ships}, \cd{f_seq}, each \cd{(B,Fc)} with
\cd{Fc=cfg.FLEET_CAP}. Per-env: \cd{ang_vel}, \cd{step_ct}, \cd{done}; plus padded official-comet
arrays \cd{c_paths/c_len/c_ships/c_slot}.

\paragraph{One tick (\texttt{env\_step}).} In order: (1) spawn comets if this is a spawn step;
(2) decode the ego action and every seat's action into launches (\cd{_decode_target} for neural
seats, \cd{opponent_action} for scripted seats); (3) deduct launched ships from origins and append
fleets into free slots; (4) add production to owned planets; (5) advance planet orbits / comet
waypoints; (6) move fleets and resolve \emph{swept} fleet--planet collisions against the planets'
\emph{moving} segments (a quadratic in the relative path), plus out-of-bounds and sun absorption;
(7) resolve combat (top-vs-second, above); (8) apply new planet positions, clear removed fleets,
increment \cd{step_ct}. \cd{env_step} returns per-env diagnostics (\cd{captured}, \cd{lost},
\cd{captured_prod}, \cd{lost_prod}, \cd{launched_ships}, \cd{owned_launch_ships}, $\ldots$) that the
reward assembly consumes.

\paragraph{Determinism.} With official comets (waypoint playback, no \cd{torch.rand}) and
deterministic seats, a tick is a pure function of state --- the property the MCTS PoC relies on for
snapshot/restore. The only stochastic seat is scripted code 0 (random heading) and the legacy
non-official comet spawner.

\section{Episodes, outcome, and seat-swap tiling}\label{sec:episodes}
\paragraph{Horizon and termination.} An episode runs \cd{cfg.EPISODE_STEPS} ticks ($=500$ full,
$200$ smoke). Per env, a rollout marks termination when \cd{step_ct}$\ge T-2$, or
$\#\text{alive}\le1$, or the ego seat is dead; finished envs are masked (no per-step CUDA-syncing
early break).

\paragraph{Outcome scalar.} At termination \cd{_scores_outcome} freezes (a) the ego's pairwise score
vs every seat ($1$ win / $0.5$ draw / $0$ loss) and (b) the scalar outcome $o\in[-1,1]$:
\[
  o = \begin{cases}
    \mathrm{sign}\!\big(s_0-\max_{p\ge1}s_p\big) & \text{2p, or 4p \cd{OUTCOME_4P="winner"}},\\[2pt]
    2\,\overline{\text{pairwise}}-1 & \text{4p \cd{OUTCOME_4P="placement"} (beat-all $=+1$).}
  \end{cases}
\]
In 2p, $o\in\{-1,0,+1\}$ exactly.

\paragraph{Seat-swap world tiling (anti positional-overfit).} Because the observation is in
\emph{absolute} board coordinates, an agent trained only as seat~0 overfits to seat-0 geometry (the
edge inverts under a $180^\circ$ board rotation; see memory \cd{seat0-tempo-advantage}). The fix is
deterministic, exhaustive seat coverage: \cd{collect_ppo} tiles $W=B/P$ distinct worlds $P$-fold and
stamps a per-block board rotation \cd{env._rot_k}$\in\{0,90,180,270\}^\circ$ (2p uses
$\{0,180\}$, 4p uses $\{0,90,180,270\}$). The board is 4-fold rotationally symmetric, so a
$k\cdot90^\circ$ rotation about the centre is an \emph{exact} seat remap; the fixed ego (pid~0) thus
plays every seat's geometry in one batch. Evaluation/recalibration builds canonical (un-rotated)
envs so ratings stay comparable.

\section{Observation encoding (\texttt{env\_encode})}
The encoder produces, for ego seat \cd{ego}, four tensors: \cd{entities} $(B,E,F)$, \cd{entity_mask}
$(B,E)$ (alive planets), \cd{action_mask} $(B,E)$ (legal sources), and \cd{globals} $(B,G)$.
Everything is computed \emph{ego-relative} so the same network plays any seat. Ownership is a
\textbf{4-channel ego-relative seat one-hot} (v9): for a body with owner $o$, channel
$r=(o-\text{ego})\bmod n_{\text{players}}$ fires; channel~0 is always ``me''; neutral is all-zero.
In 2p only channels~0--1 ever fire, so 4p reuses the identical input space.

\subsection{Per-planet threat features (180 $=6\times30$)}
For each planet, the \cd{N_THREAT_FLEETS}$=30$ \textbf{largest} inbound fleets \emph{aimed at it} are
selected by size ($|\text{ships}|\cdot2^{20}-\text{seq}$, the $-\text{seq}$ a stable tie-break); missing
slots are zero-padded. Attribution uses the \textbf{apparent} target --- the planet the fleet's ray
points at, computed by \cd{fleet_target_batch} \emph{ignoring} the sun --- so a fleet that will be
\textbf{absorbed by the sun} before arriving still shows up in the threat list of the planet it is aimed
at (the \cd{etf} below is its time-to-apparent-target). Each descriptor (6 features):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{owner one-hot (4)} --- ego-relative seat one-hot of the fleet's owner
        (channel 0 self, 1--3 the opponents in seat order; empty slots all-zero);
  \item \cd{etf} --- $\text{ETA}/\cd{THREAT_ETA_SCALE}$ (\,/100);
  \item \cd{slg} --- $|\text{ships}|$, the \textbf{raw} inbound-fleet size (un-normalized; log compression removed).
\end{itemize}
$\cd{entities}=[\text{body}\,\Vert\,\text{threat}]$ is multiplied by \cd{p_alive} so dead slots are 0.

\paragraph{v12 change (per-opponent threat identity).} Pre-v12 the fleet/body owner was a single
\emph{sign} ($+1$ self / $-1$ any-opponent), collapsing all three 4p opponents into one value. The
4-channel one-hot lets the policy attribute an incoming fleet to a specific rival; it raised
\cd{F_DIM} from 104 to 194. Older 104-feature (or 101-feature scalar-owner) members still play via
\cd{checkpoint.LegacyObsAdapter}, which down-converts the current obs at forward time
($\text{sign}=\text{ch}_0-\sum_{k=1}^3\text{ch}_k$). See memory \cd{v12-threat-ownership-onehot}.

\paragraph{Motion-aware targeting and serving.} The fleet$\to$planet attribution and \cd{etf} are
\textbf{motion-aware}: \cd{fleet_target_batch} solves the fleet-vs-\emph{moving}-planet intercept time
via a swept relative-velocity quadratic (the same form the engine's own collision uses), with each
planet's velocity taken from a one-step finite difference $\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}_{\text{prev}}$ (tracked
by the env; \emph{internal} --- never an obs feature). This leads orbiting planets and fast comets, so
a fleet aimed at where a comet \emph{will be} is attributed correctly (a static ray misses it); \cd{etf}
is the time-to-intercept in ticks. It reconstructs at serving with \textbf{no information loss}: the
official Kaggle observation gives in-flight fleets as
$[\text{id},\text{owner},x,y,\text{angle},\text{from\_pid},\text{ships}]$ and planet positions
\emph{every} turn, and the submission adapter is a persistent $B{=}1$ \cd{GpuEnv}, so the same
finite-difference velocity is available there (the v12 adapter must track previous positions, matching
planets by id). A per-source launch-ETA map fed to the WHERE head is a separate, not-yet-wired feature.

decide a capture) while the board-level summaries stay compressed.

\section{The policy network (\texttt{PolicyNet})}
\cd{build_policy(cfg)} instantiates an actor--critic that maps the four observation tensors to
WHERE/GATE actor logits, a scalar value, and (optionally) an auxiliary reward prediction. The trunk
maps \cd{entities} $(B,E,F)$ to per-planet tokens $(B,E,h)$ with $h=\cd{HIDDEN}=512$;
\cd{cfg.ARCH} selects one of three interchangeable trunks, and everything downstream is identical.

\subsection{Three trunks}
\textbf{Legacy ResNet-MLP (\cd{ARCH="trunk"}, default):}
\begin{verbatim}
  entities -> proj: Linear(F -> 512)
           -> [optional] cross-planet single-head self-attention   (USE_ATTENTION, default OFF)
           -> [optional] GLU: x = x + glu_out(glu_val(x) * sigmoid(glu_gate(x)))  (USE_GLU, ON)
           -> N_RES_BLOCKS x  pre-LN ResNet-MLP: x = x + res_b(act(res_a(LN(x))))  (=86)
           -> ln_out: LayerNorm(512)
\end{verbatim}
At the shipped defaults \cd{USE_ATTENTION} is \emph{False}, so the legacy trunk has \emph{no
cross-planet mixing inside the trunk} --- planets interact only later, at the masked-mean value pool
and the bilinear WHERE head. \cd{GRAD_CHECKPOINT} (default on) activation-checkpoints the residual
blocks so the deep ``512$\times$86'' stack fits in VRAM.

\textbf{Stacked transformer (\cd{ARCH="transformer"}):}
\begin{verbatim}
  entities -> proj: Linear(F -> 512)
           -> N_STEM_RES x pre-LN ResNet-MLP   (=1, pre-attention)
           -> N_TX_LAYERS x TxEncoderLayer     (=6 pre-LN MHA+MLP blocks)
           -> N_HEAD_RES x pre-LN ResNet-MLP   (=4, post-attention)
           -> ln_out: LayerNorm(512)
\end{verbatim}
Each \cd{TxEncoderLayer} is $x\leftarrow x+\mathrm{MHA}(\mathrm{LN}_1 x)$;
$x\leftarrow x+\mathrm{MLP}(\mathrm{LN}_2 x)$, with \cd{N_HEADS} heads (\cd{scaled_dot_product_attention},
dead-planet keys masked) and MLP expansion \cd{TX_MLP_RATIO}$=4$. \textbf{Footgun:} \cd{N_HEADS} must
divide \cd{HIDDEN}; the config default pairs $512$ with $12$ (invalid) --- a 512-wide transformer
must override \cd{N_HEADS} (e.g.\ 8, head dim 64, as \cd{INVITE_CFG} does).

\textbf{Block-sequence (\cd{ARCH="blockseq"}):} an arbitrary ordered sequence of blocks parsed from
\cd{TRUNK_SPEC}, for hand-designed depth/attention layouts:
\begin{verbatim}
  entities -> proj: Linear(F -> h)
           -> blocks from TRUNK_SPEC, in order:
                res  = pre-LN ResNet-MLP block
                attn = PURE cross-planet multi-head self-attention (pre-LN, residual, NO MLP)
                seq  = full transformer encoder layer (MHA + MLP, TX_MLP_RATIO)
           -> ln_out: LayerNorm(h)
\end{verbatim}
e.g.\ \cd{TRUNK_SPEC="res16,attn1,res64"}; \cd{GRAD_CHECKPOINT} checkpoints every block. Three preset
notebooks (\cd{make_v12_nb.py --blockseq}) ship at $h=256$, 8 heads, trained with \cd{ALGO="mc"}
(below) $+$ the win-bet aux head: \textbf{(1)} \cd{res16,attn1,res64} (81 blk, 11.3M); \textbf{(2)}
\cd{res16,attn1,res16,attn1,res32} (66 blk, 9.5M); \textbf{(3)} \cd{res8,attn1,seq16,res32}
($8$ res $\to$ attn $\to$ $16$ encoder layers $\to$ $32$ res, 57 blk, 18.7M). Each notebook carries an \emph{MCTS
depth/breadth estimate} cell (measured B=1 2-core forward $\to$ sims/turn at the $\sim$1\,s budget).

\paragraph{Critic-free Monte-Carlo policy gradient (\cd{ALGO="mc"}).} A third optimizer alongside
PPO+GAE and GRPO: near-vanilla PPO with \textbf{no critic and no GAE} --- the advantage is the
\emph{discounted Monte-Carlo return-to-go} $A_t=G_t=\sum_{k\ge t}\gamma^{\,k-t}r_k$ (reset at episode
end), batch-whitened (center $+$ global scale). It reuses the GRPO clipped-surrogate update (no value
loss), and \cd{VALUE_WARMUP_ITERS} is forced off. Computed in \cd{collect_ppo}; dispatched in
\cd{train.py}.

\subsection{Heads (shared by all trunks)}
Per planet the head input concatenates the trunk token with a board-globals embedding
$\cd{gp}=\mathrm{relu}(\cd{g_embed}(\cd{globals}))\in\R^{d_g}$, $d_g=\cd{D_G}=32$, giving
$hh\in\R^{h+d_g}=\R^{544}$.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{WHERE} (\cd{DEST_HEAD="bilinear"}, default): pointer scores
        $\cd{dest_logits}[s,d]=\langle\cd{dest_q}(hh_s),\cd{dest_k}(hh_d)\rangle/\sqrt h$, a
        $(B,E,E)$ source$\times$destination matrix.
  \item \textbf{GATE:} \cd{gate_head} Linear($544\to1$) $\Rightarrow$ per-source launch logit $(B,E)$.
  \item \textbf{VALUE (critic):} masked-mean pool of trunk tokens over live planets, concat \cd{gp},
        \cd{val_in}$\to$\cd{VALUE_RES_BLOCKS}$=4$ pre-LN ResNet-MLP blocks$\to$\cd{val_out}. Targets
        are \textbf{PopArt}-normalized (output-preserving running mean/var; the head predicts
        normalized values, raw $V=\text{denorm}$). The critic is used by PPO; GRPO leaves it
        untrained (unless \cd{GRPO_AUX_VALUE}); the MCTS \cd{value="net"} mode reads it.
  \item \textbf{AUX reward} (optional, \cd{AUX_REWARD_PRED}, default off): a shallow
        \cd{rew_in}$\to$act$\to$\cd{rew_out} off the same pooled features (document~2). Built only
        when enabled, so checkpoints are byte-identical when off.
\end{itemize}

\section{Action space \& distributions}\label{sec:action}
The actor is a \textbf{gated per-source allocation}. For every owned source planet it emits a WHERE
distribution over destinations and a launch GATE. \emph{On fire, the planet dispatches its full
garrison routed by the WHERE row; on no-fire it holds.} The action bundle is $(B,E,E{+}1)$:
$[\,{:}E\,]$ the allocation row, $[\,E\,]$ fire $\in\{0,1\}$. Log-prob and entropy \emph{sum over
owned sources}; the WHERE term only counts when firing.

\paragraph{Masking.} WHERE logits are clamped to $\pm\cd{LOGIT_CLAMP}=8$ then masked to $-10^9$ on:
the diagonal (self), dead destinations (\cd{alive}$<0.5$), and sun-blocked destinations
(\cd{reach}$<0.5$ --- a straight shot absorbed by the sun, recovered geometrically by
\cd{compute_reach} identically in rollout and update). The support is exactly the legal, reachable
targets.

\paragraph{Gate.} $g=\sigma(\cd{gate_logit})$. \textbf{Train:} $p=g$ (smooth, full-support gradient
--- the deploy deadzone clamp used to zero it and vanish the gate gradient). \textbf{Deploy:} hard
deadzone $p=\mathrm{clip}((g-\cd{GATE_TRIM_LO})/(\cd{GATE_TRIM_HI}-\cd{GATE_TRIM_LO}),0,1)$. Then
$p\leftarrow\mathrm{clip}(p,\cd{GATE_EPS},1-\cd{GATE_EPS})$, $\text{fire}\sim\text{Bernoulli}(p)$.

\paragraph{\texttt{GatedCatDist} (categorical WHERE, \cd{WHERE_DIST="categorical"}, default).} After
masking, $\cd{lsm}=\log\mathrm{softmax}(\cd{glogits})$. \emph{Sample:} dest $\sim$ Categorical,
fire $\sim$ Bernoulli. \emph{Log-prob (per source):}
$\ell=[\text{fire}\log p+(1{-}\text{fire})\log(1{-}p)]+\text{fire}\cdot\cd{lsm}[\text{dest}]$, summed
over owned sources. \emph{Entropy:} Bernoulli gate entropy $+\cd{ENT_DIR_COEF}\cdot p\cdot H_{\text{cat}}$
with $H_{\text{cat}}\le\ln E$; \cd{ENT_DIR_COEF}$=0$ by default, so the bonus is \emph{gate-only}. The
bounded-sensitivity $\log\mathrm{softmax}$ log-prob is what makes PPO-after-BC stable.

\paragraph{\texttt{GatedAllocDist} (Dirichlet WHERE, v6 A/B).} $\alpha=\mathrm{softplus}(\text{logits})\cdot
\cd{ALLOC_KAPPA}+10^{-3}$; rows $\sim$ Dirichlet, splitting the garrison across destinations. Its
greedy decode uses a temperature-sharpened softmax ($\tau=\cd{GREEDY_TAU}=0.5$) because the
Dirichlet mean spreads the garrison too thin to land a concentrated strike.

\paragraph{Decode (\texttt{\_decode\_target}).} A fired source's full garrison $S$ is routed by its
renormalised off-diagonal WHERE row $\hat A$: $n_{s,d}=\lfloor \hat A_{s,d}\,S_s\rfloor$ to alive,
legal destinations, kept only if $n\ge\cd{MIN_LAUNCH_SHIPS}=2$ (dribbles stay home --- a structural
budget, no penalty). Each launch is aimed at the orbiting intercept. So although the categorical
WHERE samples one destination, the \emph{decode} is a deterministic full-garrison strike to that
target.

\section{Reward (used by the RL trainers)}\label{sec:reward}
The reward is assembled inside \cd{collect_ppo} (\cd{rollout.py}) and consumed by PPO+GAE and GRPO;
the MCTS PoC does not use it. Two per-step regimes (selected by \cd{USE_POTENTIAL_SHAPING}, default
\emph{off} --- the small legacy dense channels, so the terminal W/L dominates) feed a per-step $r_t$,
plus a sparse terminal payment; everything is divided by \cd{PPO_REWARD_SCALE}$=100$ before it
reaches the learner.

\subsection{Optional: potential-based shaping}
Policy-invariant shaping (Ng et al.) on two potentials, evaluated by \cd{_potentials} as the ego's
log-ship margin and production share vs the \emph{strongest} opponent:
\[
  \Phi_{\text{ship}} = \cd{ship_log_t}(s_0)-\cd{ship_log_t}\!\big(\max_{p\ge1}s_p\big),
  \qquad
  \Phi_{\text{prod}} = \frac{P_0-\max_{p\ge1}P_p}{\sum P}.
\]
Per step (for still-active envs),
\[
  r_t = \cd{SHAPE_SHIP}\,(\gamma\Phi_{\text{ship}}'-\Phi_{\text{ship}})
      + \cd{SHAPE_PROD}\,(\gamma\Phi_{\text{prod}}'-\Phi_{\text{prod}}),
  \qquad \cd{SHAPE_SHIP}=\cd{SHAPE_PROD}=25,\ \gamma=\cd{GAMMA}=0.997,
\]
where primes are the post-step potentials. Being a $\gamma$-difference of a potential, this shaping
is policy-invariant (it cannot change the optimal policy); it only densifies credit.

\subsection{Default: legacy dense channels}
With \cd{USE_POTENTIAL_SHAPING=False} (the default), $r_t$ is the sum of three measured channels:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{capture:} $\cd{CAPTURE_REWARD}\big[(\text{captured}+\cd{CAPTURE_PROD_SCALE}\,\text{captured\_prod})
        -\cd{CAPTURE_LOSS_FRAC}(\text{lost}+\cd{CAPTURE_PROD_SCALE}\,\text{lost\_prod})\big]$,
        $\cd{CAPTURE_REWARD}=30$, comet flips excluded;
  \item \textbf{production milestone:} $+\cd{PROD_MILESTONE_REWARD}=20$ each time total strength
        doubles past \cd{PROD_MILESTONE_BASE}$=100$ (monotone --- losing ships never claws it back);
  \item \textbf{launch shaping} (first \cd{LAUNCH_WINDOW}$=150$ steps): a small per-ship-launched
        bonus, per-step- and per-game-capped (\cd{LAUNCH_STEP_CAP}, \cd{LAUNCH_GAME_CAP}); unspent
        budget is cashed out on a win.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Terminal outcome payment}
Both regimes add the sparse game result to the \emph{last active step}. With episode length $\ell$
and $\text{len\_eff}=(\ell-\cd{DECAY_START_STEP})_+$, $\cd{DECAY_START_STEP}=100$,
\[
  O = \begin{cases}
    o\cdot\cd{WIN_DECAY}^{\,\text{len\_eff}}\cdot\cd{WIN_BONUS}, & o>0,\\
    o\cdot\cd{LOSS_DECAY}^{\,\text{len\_eff}}\cdot\cd{LOSS_PENALTY}, & o\le0,
  \end{cases}
  \qquad \cd{WIN_BONUS}=\cd{LOSS_PENALTY}=1000,
\]
added as $O/\cd{PPO_REWARD_SCALE}$. In 4p placement mode the fractional $o$ scales the magnitudes
linearly; in 2p $o\in\{-1,0,+1\}$.

\paragraph{Outcome decay.} The current defaults are $\cd{WIN_DECAY}=1.0$ and
$\cd{LOSS_DECAY}=0.9995$: an asymmetric ``lose-slower'' shaping (a late loss is penalized slightly
less than an early one --- a mild survival incentive), with wins undecayed. The provenance doc
\cd{set-ups/1.md} used a $0.995$ symmetric decay past step~100. This is purely a reward-shaping knob:
an earlier write-up blamed an asymmetric decay for the GRPO passivity collapse, but that did not hold
up --- the collapse is caused by the advantage-estimator changes (document~3), not by the decay.

\section{Self-play league (the curriculum)}\label{sec:league}
There are \emph{no hand-coded curriculum stages}: the dual-Elo PFSP league (\cd{league.py}) \emph{is}
the curriculum. Each iteration samples an opponent (or a 4p trio), the learner rolls out against it,
and the result updates ratings. The two RL trainers share this verbatim; the MCTS PoC does not use
it.

\paragraph{Members and ratings.} Every member carries a 2p rating \cd{elo} and a 4p rating \cd{elo4}.
Fixed scripted \emph{anchors} seed the league --- \cd{random}, \cd{starter}, \cd{greedy},
\cd{medium}, \cd{intermediate} --- at pinned ratings (e.g.\ \cd{ELO_STARTER}$=1350$,
\cd{ELO_MEDIUM}$=1560$). Frozen learner \emph{snapshots} (added every \cd{SELFPLAY_REFRESH} iters)
and \emph{invited} external agents fill the rest. The learner itself is grounded on a \textbf{rolling
self-anchor} --- its own frozen high-water snapshot --- so its rating never clamps on saturated
scripts (memory \cd{v9-elo-saturation-confirmed}).

\paragraph{Scripted opponents (\texttt{opponent\_action}).} The anchor ``kinds'' map to deterministic
(except code~0) rules of escalating strength:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llL{0.56\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
code & kind & behaviour (one launch per owned planet, half garrison $\ge20$) \\
\midrule
0 & random       & random heading \\
1 & starter      & fire at the nearest \emph{static} non-owned planet \\
2 & noop         & never launches \\
3 & medium       & nearest non-owned planet incl.\ orbiting, lead-aimed at the intercept \\
4 & greedy       & nearest beatable non-owned planet within \cd{CAPTURE_RADIUS}, just-enough ships \\
5 & intermediate & in-flight-aware capture from the nearest capable source $+$ counter $+$ rebalance $+$ comet-escape \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{PFSP matchmaking.} Per-member sampling weight (\cd{_weights}) is a PFSP function of the
expected score $p$ (\cd{PFSP_MODE="even"}: $p(1{-}p)$ --- evenly-matched opponents) times a
behavioural-footprint mix (2p), invited/scripted boosts, and an advance lock (\cd{greedy}/\cd{medium}
stay locked until the learner clears \cd{ADVANCE_TRIGGER_ELO}). A scripted-share cap keeps scripts
collectively $\le\cd{SCRIPT_SHARE_CAP}=0.30$ while any neural member carries weight; a 2p-mastered
script leaves 2p matchmaking.

\paragraph{Dual Elo and the 2p/4p mix.} 2p results are a single Elo update; 4p FFA results are
decomposed into pairwise updates at \cd{ELO_K4}/$k$ each, cross-coupled into the 2p rating by
\cd{ELO_COUPLING}$=0.25$. The fraction of 4p iterations is \cd{share_4p} from an EMA of the
(elo2$-$elo4) gap, \emph{pinned} to $0.25$ at the shipped defaults
($\cd{S4_MIN}=\cd{S4_MAX}=0.25$, a 3:1 2p:4p ratio; memory \cd{share4p-pinned-1to1}).

\paragraph{Recalibration and measured eviction.} Periodic recals re-ground both rating scales on the
top self-anchor and blend into the live ratings (first recal is raw). A \textbf{measured eviction}
(\cd{evict_mastered}) removes any league member the learner has mastered under one standard --- 2p
seat-averaged score $>\cd{MASTER_EVICT_2P_WR}=0.90$ \emph{or} 4p 1st-place rate vs three copies
$>\cd{MASTER_EVICT_4P_WR}=0.75$ --- covering scripted anchors and neural players alike, but never the
self-anchors, the newest snapshot, or the \cd{random}/\cd{greedy} watchdogs. Dormant
\emph{watchdogs} (\cd{random}, \cd{greedy}) re-enter as live opponents if the learner underperforms
its own rating prediction by \cd{WATCHDOG_TOL} --- a collapse alarm. See memory
\cd{v12-clean-league-measured-eviction} and \cd{v12-eviction-league-players}.

\paragraph{Best-checkpoint selection.} Checkpoints are scored on a held-out world pool by a
\emph{mixed gauntlet}: a 2p score vs (\cd{starter}, \cd{intermediate}, \cd{greedy}) blended with a 4p
FFA score (scripted trio $+$ invited-neural trio), weighted by \cd{GAUNTLET_4P_W}. The held-out seeds
are never touched by training, so the score measures generalization and stays comparable across the
run.

\subsection{Behaviour-cloning warm-start (\texttt{bc\_pretrain})}
From-scratch RL reliably falls into a passivity trap (memory
\cd{orbit-wars-policy-failure-diagnosis}), so training optionally starts from a BC clone of the
\cd{medium} rule. The clone visits its own states against a mixed scripted-opponent schedule and
minimizes $\text{BCE}(\text{gate})+\text{CE}(\text{masked WHERE})$ on firing sources, expressing the
expert as one-hot WHERE rows $+$ fire. This produces a committing, aiming policy (memory
\cd{bc-warmstart-gated-alloc}); both RL trainers then resume from it.

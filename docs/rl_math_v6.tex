\section{Policy network: heads}\label{v6:net}
The \textbf{trunk is unchanged} --- input projection, one (or $N_{\mathrm{tx}}$) masked cross-planet
self-attention block(s), GLU/residual-MLP stack, final LayerNorm, separate globals embedding $g'$:
\rlref{Policy network}{sec:policy}, including the trunk-vs-transformer sizing of \rlref{Recommended
hyperparameters}{sec:trunkcfg}. v6 changes only the \emph{heads} and one attention \emph{kernel}.

\paragraph{(a) Attention kernel (math-neutral).} The attention of Eq.~(\texttt{sec:policy}) is computed
with \cd{F.scaled\_dot\_product\_attention} (a fused flash/mem-efficient kernel) under a bf16 autocast;
the dead-planet key mask is supplied as an additive $-10^9$ bias. Identical function, faster kernel.

\paragraph{(b) Bilinear (pointer) destination head.} \texttt{rl\_math.tex} uses a single
\cd{Linear}$(d{+}d_g\!\to\!E)$ for the \cd{dest\_head}. v6 replaces it (flag \cd{DEST\_HEAD="bilinear"})
with a pairwise pointer score over the source/destination tokens $h'_e=[h_e;g']\in\R^{d+d_g}$:
\begin{equation}
\ell_{s,d} \;=\; \frac{\big(W_Q\,h'_s\big)^{\!\top}\big(W_K\,h'_d\big)}{\sqrt{d}}\,,\qquad
W_Q,W_K\in\R^{d\times(d+d_g)} .
\end{equation}
The score for sending $s\!\to\!d$ now depends on \emph{both} planets' features (relative geometry,
ownership, garrison, threat), instead of a fixed per-slot weight vector --- the natural inductive bias for
a relational allocation. \emph{Calm init:} $W_Q,W_K$ scaled by $\cd{INIT\_DEST\_SCALE}=0.02$ so
$\ell\approx 0$ (near-uniform WHERE) at start; ``hold by default'' is enforced by the gate bias below, not
by a diagonal logit bias.

\paragraph{(c) Launch-gate head.} v6 adds \cd{gate\_head}: a \cd{Linear}$(d{+}d_g\!\to\!1)$ emitting a
per-source fire logit $\eta_s$ (\S\ref{v6:dist}). Bias initialized to $\cd{INIT\_GATE\_BIAS}=-1$ (few
planets fire at start).

\paragraph{(d) Value head predicts a \emph{normalized} value (PopArt).} The critic architecture is
unchanged (masked-mean pool of trunk tokens $\,\oplus\,g'\to$ \cd{VALUE\_RES\_BLOCKS} pre-LN residual
blocks $\to d{\to}1$; \rlref{PPO\,+\,GAE}{sec:ppo}). In v6 its scalar output is the \emph{normalized}
value $\tilde V_\theta(s)$; the usable value is $V(s)=\sigma\,\tilde V_\theta(s)+\mu$ with the running
$(\mu,\sigma)$ of \S\ref{v6:popart}. GAE and bootstrapping use the de-normalized $V$.

\section{Action distribution: gated allocation}\label{v6:dist}
\texttt{rl\_math.tex} \S\,\texttt{sec:dist} makes each owned source row a Dirichlet over the destination
simplex with the \emph{self-diagonal as} \cd{HOLD}, and evaluates greedily at the Dirichlet \emph{mean}.
v6 keeps the Dirichlet for \emph{where} but adds an explicit \textbf{Bernoulli launch gate} for
\emph{whether}, and replaces the greedy rule. The reachability mask $R_{s,j}$ (sun occlusion) and the
owned-source factorization are exactly as in \texttt{sec:dist}.

\paragraph{Two factors per owned source $s$.}
\begin{align}
\textbf{WHERE: }\quad & \alpha_{s,j}=\softplus\!\big(\clip(\ell_{s,j},\pm \cd{LOGIT\_CLAMP})\big)\cdot\cd{ALLOC\_KAPPA}+\epsilon,
   \qquad A[s,\cdot]\sim\mathrm{Dirichlet}(\alpha_s),\\[2pt]
\textbf{WHETHER: }\quad & g_s=\sigma(\eta_s),\quad
   p_s=\clip\!\Big(\tfrac{g_s-\cd{GATE\_TRIM\_LO}}{\cd{GATE\_TRIM\_HI}-\cd{GATE\_TRIM\_LO}},\,0,1\Big),\quad
   p_s\leftarrow\clip(p_s,\cd{GATE\_EPS},1-\cd{GATE\_EPS}),\\
   & f_s\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(p_s)\in\{0,1\}.
\end{align}
The trim $(\cd{GATE\_TRIM\_LO},\cd{GATE\_TRIM\_HI})=(0.2,0.8)$ gives a dead-zone / saturation band so the
gate can express ``essentially never/always fire''; the final clip to $[\cd{GATE\_EPS},1{-}\cd{GATE\_EPS}]$,
$\cd{GATE\_EPS}=0.02$, keeps a saturated gate \emph{trainable} (nonzero gradient) during sampling. The
sampled action is bundled as the $(B,E,E{+}1)$ tensor $a=[\,A\,|\,f\,]$.

\paragraph{Log-prob and entropy (the WHERE term is gated by firing).}
\begin{align}
\log\pol(a\mid s) &= \!\!\sum_{s:\,\text{owned}}\!\!\Big[\,\underbrace{f_s\log p_s+(1{-}f_s)\log(1{-}p_s)}_{\text{Bernoulli gate}}
   \;+\; f_s\,\log\mathrm{Dirichlet}\big(A[s,\cdot]\mid\alpha_s\big)\,\Big],\\
\mathcal H[\pol(\cdot\mid s)] &= \!\!\sum_{s:\,\text{owned}}\!\!\Big[\,\mathcal H_{\mathrm{Bern}}(p_s)
   \;+\; p_s\,\mathcal H_{\mathrm{Dir}}(\alpha_s)\,\Big].
\end{align}
The WHERE log-prob counts \emph{only} when the planet fired ($f_s{=}1$): on a hold the destination row is
unobserved, so it must not contribute to the score (otherwise a holding planet would be credited/penalized
for an allocation it never executed). Symmetrically the WHERE entropy is weighted by the fire probability
$p_s$.

\paragraph{Decode (\rlref{Decode}{sec:decode} with the gate).} A firing source dispatches its
\textbf{full garrison} routed by its renormalized off-diagonal row; a holding source keeps everything:
\begin{equation}
\hat A[s,d]=\frac{A[s,d]\,(1-\delta_{sd})}{\sum_{d'\neq s}A[s,d']},\qquad
n_{s,d}=\big\lfloor \hat A[s,d]\,S_s\,f_s\big\rfloor\ \ \text{for alive } d,\ \ n_{s,d}\ge\cd{MIN\_LAUNCH\_SHIPS}.
\end{equation}
Aiming is the orbital intercept of \texttt{sec:lead}. Because the row is a simplex and $f_s\in\{0,1\}$,
total dispatch is $0$ (hold) or exactly the garrison spread over destinations (fire); over-dispatch is
structurally impossible, as in \texttt{sec:dist}.

\paragraph{Greedy / deploy --- the v6 change.} \texttt{rl\_math.tex} deploys at the Dirichlet
\emph{mean}, which is concentration-blind: a moderately-preferred row ($\ell$ gap $\sim$2) has mean mass
$\approx\softplus(2)/\!\sum\softplus\approx0.06$ on the preferred destination, so the full garrison spreads
thin and \emph{every} $\lfloor\cdot\rfloor$ floors below \cd{MIN\_LAUNCH\_SHIPS} $\Rightarrow$ zero
launches (a residual greedy-passive failure that the gate alone does not fix). v6 deploys with a
\textbf{temperature-sharpened, masked softmax} over the live, sun-reachable, \emph{off-diagonal}
destinations and a hard gate:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;A^{\mathrm{greedy}}[s,\cdot]=\softmax\!\Big(\tilde\ell_s/\tau\Big),\quad
\tilde\ell_{s,j}=\ell_{s,j}\ \text{if}\ (j\neq s)\wedge m_j\wedge R_{s,j},\ \text{else } -\infty;\qquad
f_s^{\mathrm{greedy}}=\mathbb 1[p_s\ge 0.5]\;}
\end{equation}
with $\tau=\cd{GREEDY\_TAU}=0.5$. This concentrates the garrison on the best valid destination (a real
strike) instead of dust, and never wastes mass on dead/unreachable/self slots. \emph{Verified:} in the
moderate-preference regime the mean greedy fires $0$ launches while the sharpened greedy fires one
concentrated strike per firing source. Sampling is unchanged (the unmasked Dirichlet $+$ Bernoulli; the
decode and the natural sun-occlusion signal still apply).

\section{Reward and return: potential-based shaping}\label{v6:reward}
\texttt{rl\_math.tex} \S\,\texttt{sec:reward} uses a four-channel \emph{event} reward
$R=O+C+D+P$ (outcome, capture, dispatch, production-milestone). Those dense channels are
\emph{farmable}: capture rewards border-flip churn, dispatch/milestone reward ship expenditure/hoarding
--- all maximizable while \emph{losing}. v6 (flag \cd{USE\_POTENTIAL\_SHAPING}, default on)
\textbf{replaces $C,D,P$} with \textbf{potential-based shaping} (Ng, Harada \& Russell, 1999), keeping the
outcome $O$ exactly as in \texttt{rl\_math.tex} Eq.~(\texttt{eq:return}). The legacy four-channel reward is retained verbatim
behind \cd{USE\_POTENTIAL\_SHAPING=False} ($\equiv$ \texttt{sec:reward}).

\paragraph{Potentials.} With per-side total ships $S^0,S^1$ (planets $+$ fleets), the ship-log
$\ell(n)=\ln(1{+}n)/\ln 1000$, per-side owned production $\Pi^0,\Pi^1$ and total alive production
$\Pi_{\mathrm{tot}}$,
\begin{equation}
\Phi_{\mathrm{ship}}(s)=\ell(S^0)-\ell(S^1),\qquad
\Phi_{\mathrm{prod}}(s)=\frac{\Pi^0-\Pi^1}{\max(\Pi_{\mathrm{tot}},1)} .
\end{equation}
Both are zero-sum, bounded ($\Phi_{\mathrm{ship}}\in[-1,1]$, $\Phi_{\mathrm{prod}}\in[-1,1]$), and
directly track the win condition (ship margin / board-control margin).

\paragraph{Shaping reward.} The per-step reward is the shaping form
\begin{equation}
r_t \;=\; c_{\mathrm{ship}}\big(\gamma\,\Phi_{\mathrm{ship}}(s_{t+1})-\Phi_{\mathrm{ship}}(s_t)\big)
        + c_{\mathrm{prod}}\big(\gamma\,\Phi_{\mathrm{prod}}(s_{t+1})-\Phi_{\mathrm{prod}}(s_t)\big),
\label{v6:shape}
\end{equation}
with $c_{\mathrm{ship}}=\cd{SHAPE\_SHIP}=50$, $c_{\mathrm{prod}}=\cd{SHAPE\_PROD}=50$, the \emph{same}
$\gamma$ as GAE. As in \texttt{sec:reward} the outcome $O$ is scattered onto the last active step, and
both $r_t$ and $O$ are divided by $\sigma_R=\cd{PPO\_REWARD\_SCALE}=100$ before GAE.

\paragraph{Policy invariance (why this is un-hackable).} For $F(s,s')=\gamma\Phi(s')-\Phi(s)$ the added
discounted return telescopes,
\begin{equation}
\sum_{t\ge0}\gamma^t F(s_t,s_{t+1}) \;=\; \gamma^{T}\Phi(s_T)-\Phi(s_0),
\end{equation}
which is independent of the policy (only of the fixed start state and the terminal). Hence the optimal
policy and ordering of policies are \emph{unchanged} versus training on the sparse outcome $O$ alone, while
the gradient is now dense and win-aligned at every step. No reward-farming basin exists: a quantity that
returns to its prior value (e.g.\ a flip-flopped border planet) contributes net zero. Empirically the
outcome channel dominates the per-game return (e.g.\ $O\!\sim\!450$ vs.\ shaping $\sim\!8$ each), the
intended regime.

\paragraph{Minor: comet-masked capture.} In the legacy reward mode the capture channel now excludes comet
slots (a comet that expires while ego-owned was previously miscounted as a lost planet). No effect in
shaping mode.

\section{Curriculum and league}\label{v6:league}
Mechanics unchanged: \rlref{Curriculum}{sec:curriculum} (the Elo league \emph{is} the curriculum) and
\rlref{Self-play league}{sec:league}. Two changes:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \textbf{Best-checkpoint selection = fixed-opponent gauntlet.} \texttt{rl\_math.tex}/the prior
        notebook saved ``best'' on the per-iter win-rate \emph{vs.\ whatever single PFSP opponent was
        drawn} (a weak net that drew \cd{random} could be frozen as best). v6 selects on a
        deployment-faithful, opponent-\emph{independent} gauntlet: greedy play vs.\ a fixed anchor set
        $\{\cd{starter},\cd{intermediate},\cd{greedy}\}$, scored every \cd{CKPT\_EVERY} iters,
        $g=\frac1{|\mathcal A|}\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\mathrm{score}(\pi,a)$.
  \item \textbf{Fingerprint probe on real states.} The behavioral fingerprint used by PFSP diversity is
        computed on a cached batch of \emph{real} encoded game states (rolled out), not Gaussian-noise
        inputs, so the destination-distribution distance measures on-manifold behavior.
\end{enumerate}

\section{PPO\,+\,GAE: knobs, KL early-stop, PopArt}\label{v6:ppo}
The actor--critic PPO\,+\,GAE objective, GAE recursion, and value loss are as in
\rlref{Appendix: PPO\,+\,GAE}{sec:ppo}. v6 changes the following.

\paragraph{Horizon and sample reuse.} $\gamma=\cd{GAMMA}=0.997$ (was $0.99$) and
$\lambda=\cd{GAE\_LAMBDA}=0.98$ (was $0.95$): a $\sim$500-step game with $\gamma{=}0.99$ discounts the
terminal to $0.99^{500}\!\approx\!7\!\times\!10^{-3}$ and confines GAE credit to the last
$\sim\!1/(1{-}\gamma\lambda)$ steps; raising both lets the (now win-aligned) signal reach mid-game
actions. \cd{UPDATE\_EPOCHS}$=3$ (was $1$) recovers real multi-epoch PPO sample reuse (one epoch is
A2C-with-GAE).

\paragraph{KL early-stop (end-of-epoch).} The prior code broke the update on the first \emph{minibatch}
whose $k_3$ KL exceeded \cd{KL\_TARGET} (a high-variance, order-dependent, subsample-biased stop). v6
(\cd{KL\_EARLYSTOP}) instead completes each epoch and stops \emph{after} an epoch if its \emph{mean}
minibatch KL exceeds \cd{KL\_TARGET}$=0.05$, a lower-variance trust-region control.

\paragraph{Entropy normalization (scale-correct).} The entropy bonus is the mean \emph{per owned source}
entropy,
\begin{equation}
\overline{\mathcal H}=\frac1{|\mathcal B|}\sum_{b\in\mathcal B}\frac{\mathcal H_b}{\max(n^{b}_{\mathrm{owned}},1)},
\qquad n^{b}_{\mathrm{owned}}=\textstyle\sum_e \cd{action\_mask}^{b}_e ,
\end{equation}
replacing the prior $\overline{\mathcal H}=\mathcal H/(E\cdot\cd{nap})$ with $\cd{nap}{=}2$ (a stale
constant from the retired $(\text{dest},\phi)$ actor whose magnitude drifted with how many planets the
ego owned). The coefficient $c_{\mathcal H}$ is \textbf{annealed} $\cd{ENT\_COEF\_START}=0.02\to
\cd{ENT\_COEF\_END}=0.002$ over \cd{ENT\_DECAY\_ITERS}$=300$ (explore $\to$ exploit), vs.\ a fixed
\cd{ent\_coef}.

\paragraph{Terminal-correct GAE \cd{done}.} The episode \cd{done} flag is now set at \emph{any} terminal
--- both true elimination \emph{and} the time cap --- so $d_t=\mathbb 1[\text{cap}\vee\text{death}]$. With
the outcome scattered onto each env's last active step, this prevents a spurious $\gamma V(s_T)$ bootstrap
being added on top of the terminal outcome for time-capped games (the prior code set \cd{done} only on
death).

\paragraph{PopArt value normalization.}\label{v6:popart} The MSE critic target spans a huge dynamic range
(the $\pm1000/\sigma_R$ terminal spike vs.\ $O(10^{-2})$ per-step shaping). v6 (\cd{USE\_POPART}) trains
the value head in a \emph{normalized} space with output-preserving rescaling (Hessel et al., 2018). With
running statistics over GAE returns $\hat R$, EMA rate $\beta=\cd{POPART\_BETA}=3\!\times\!10^{-4}$,
\begin{equation}
\mu\leftarrow(1{-}\beta)\mu+\beta\,\E[\hat R],\quad
\nu\leftarrow(1{-}\beta)\nu+\beta\,\E[\hat R^2],\quad
\sigma=\sqrt{\nu-\mu^2},
\end{equation}
the value loss regresses the normalized target,
$\mathcal L^{V}=\big(\tilde V_\theta(s_t)-(\hat R_t-\mu)/\sigma\big)^2$, while GAE/bootstrapping use the
de-normalized $V=\sigma\tilde V_\theta+\mu$. On each statistics update $(\mu,\sigma)\!\to\!(\mu',\sigma')$
the final value layer is rescaled to \emph{preserve} raw outputs,
\begin{equation}
W\leftarrow W\,\frac{\sigma}{\sigma'},\qquad b\leftarrow\frac{\sigma\,b+\mu-\mu'}{\sigma'},
\end{equation}
so $V(s)=\sigma\tilde V_\theta(s)+\mu$ is invariant to the update (verified to $4\times10^{-7}$). PopArt
state is persisted in the resumable train-state.

\paragraph{Everything else.} The clipped surrogate (\texttt{rl\_math.tex} \texttt{eq:clip}), the log-ratio clamp, \cd{vf\_coef},
\cd{MINIBATCHES}, \cd{MAX\_GRAD\_NORM}, and the KL definitions are unchanged
(\rlref{KL / trust region}{sec:kldiv}). \cd{kl\_beta}$=0$ (no reference-policy anchor) for the from-scratch run.

\section{Systems / data path (A100, compute-bound)}\label{v6:sys}
The simulator \emph{dynamics} are byte-identical to \rlref{Batched GPU simulator}{sec:gpuenv}; v6 only
changes how the rollout and update move data so the GPU is the bottleneck (no math change):
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \textbf{bf16 autocast} on every net forward (rollout \emph{and} update); the distribution
        log-prob / entropy / value and the GAE remain fp32. bf16 has fp32 range, so \textbf{no GradScaler}
        --- safe against the Dirichlet \cd{log\_prob}.
  \item \textbf{On-GPU rollout buffers $+$ GPU GAE}: the per-step $(\text{ent},\text{mask},\text{act},\log\pi,V,r,\text{done})$
        buffers live on-device and the GAE recursion runs on-GPU --- no host copies, no
        \cd{.cpu()}/\cd{.item()}/\cd{synchronize} in the step loop. The only removed CPU sync (the per-step
        comet-schedule \cd{.item()} and the early-break) is replaced by passing the Python step index.
  \item \textbf{Flash SDPA} attention kernel (\S\ref{v6:net}a) and \textbf{fused Adam}.
  \item Minibatch tensors are indexed \emph{on-device} in the update (no per-minibatch host$\to$device copy).
  \item Batch $B=256$ (\cd{NUM\_GROUPS}$\times$\cd{GROUP\_SIZE}$=16\times16$) for an A100; memory is
        dominated by the rollout buffers ($\approx\!4$\,GB at $B{=}256,T{=}500$), comfortably within a 40/80\,GB A100.
\end{itemize}

\section{Appendix: model scaling (transformer)}\label{v6:scaling}
Since v6 runs no MCTS, the net \emph{is} the agent and the binding constraint on an A100 is wall-clock
$\times$ iterations, not memory. A scaling check is a \emph{ladder} of runs differing only in the
transformer stack, all else fixed (seed, reward, league, $B$, $T$), compared on the gauntlet score of
\S\ref{v6:league} vs.\ both iteration and wall-clock. With per-layer parameter count $\approx 12d^2$ and
per-iter forward cost $\propto (12 N_{\mathrm{tx}}{+}c)\,d^2$ (dominated by the $T$ sequential rollout
forwards), a geometric ladder is e.g.
\[
\text{S }(d{=}256,N_{\mathrm{tx}}{=}4)\ \to\ \text{M }(384,6)\ \to\ \text{L }(512,8)\ \to\ \text{XL }(768,12),
\quad \approx 4\text{M}\to12\text{M}\to28\text{M}\to90\text{M params},
\]
with $\cd{N\_HEADS}=d/64$. RL scaling is not guaranteed monotonic (bigger nets are more sample-hungry and
less stable; use LR warmup and lower LR for L/XL); read the gauntlet-vs-wall-clock curve for the
compute-optimal size, and keep the deployable size within the Kaggle CPU turn budget.

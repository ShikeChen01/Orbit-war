\section{The critic-free estimator and the collapse it produced}\label{sec:setup}
\begin{varsheet}
$B$ & parallel envs per iteration, $B=n_W\!\cdot\!G$ \\
$G$ & \emph{group size}: rollouts sharing one world (\cd{GRPO\_GROUP}); $n_W$ = groups/iter \\
$w_g$ & the world (initial planets/seating) shared by all $G$ members of group $g$ \\
$R_i$ & rollout $i$'s scalar return (full shaped return, or terminal-only if \cd{GRPO\_OUTCOME\_ONLY}) \\
$O_i$ & terminal outcome of rollout $i$; in 2p $O_i\in\{-1,0,+1\}$, in 4p the placement-linear value \\
$\mu_g,\sigma_g$ & within-group sample mean / std of $\{R_i\}$ \\
$\Adv_i$ & the advantage assigned to \emph{every} timestep of rollout $i$ \\
$\varepsilon$ & \cd{GRPO\_ADV\_EPS}, the std floor ($10^{-4}$) \\
$\pol$ & the policy; $a^i_t,s^i_t$ the action/state at step $t$ of rollout $i$ \\
\end{varsheet}
GRPO replaces PPO's learned value baseline with a \textbf{group baseline}. Each iteration draws
$n_W$ worlds and rolls each out $G$ times (the rollout lays the $B$ envs out as contiguous blocks of
$G$, one world per block, so a block is a group). With the return $R_i$ standardized inside its
group,
\begin{equation}
\Adv_i=\frac{R_i-\mu_g}{\sigma_g+\varepsilon},\qquad
\mu_g=\tfrac1G\!\sum_{j\in g}\!R_j,\quad
\sigma_g=\sqrt{\tfrac1G\!\sum_{j\in g}\!(R_j-\mu_g)^2},
\label{eq:grpo}
\end{equation}
the gradient is the clipped surrogate of the PPO section applied with $\Adv_i$ \emph{flat over all
timesteps} of rollout $i$:
$\;\hat g=\E\big[\sum_t\nabla_\theta\log\pol(a^i_t\mid s^i_t)\,\Adv_i\big]$. There is no critic, no
GAE, and (with \cd{GRPO\_KL\_COEF}$=0$) no reference anchor.

\paragraph{The collapse.} A warm-started run climbed to $\mathrm{elo2}\approx2110$, then over $\sim$40
iterations decayed to win-rate $0.09$ with the launch rate sliding $0.80\to0.30$ --- the project's
familiar \emph{passivity basin}. The trigger was an asymmetric outcome decay (a small ``lose slower''
term), but the \emph{amplifier} was Eq.~\eqref{eq:grpo} itself: in a group that loses every rollout,
$\sigma_g$ collapses and the $\varepsilon$-floored division blows a trivial within-group tiebreak up to
a full-magnitude ``stall'' gradient. Sections~\ref{sec:A}--\ref{sec:B} dissect and remove that
amplifier; Section~\ref{sec:C} restores the per-step credit assignment GRPO structurally lacks;
Section~\ref{sec:D} tightens the baseline. All four are properties of \emph{this} MDP: a
terminal-dominated, near-binary reward evaluated on shared-world groups under self-play.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Empirical reversal (Section~\ref{sec:empirical}, added after the ablation).}
The framing above is half wrong. Removing the ``amplifier'' (Sections~\ref{sec:A}, \ref{sec:B},
\ref{sec:rank}) turned out to be the \emph{wrong} fix --- it causes passivity, because the std-norm
rare-win amplification is double-edged: it is also what keeps the agent \emph{aggressive}. The right fix
is to remove the \emph{trigger} (keep the outcome decay symmetric); the identical legacy std-norm is stable
without it. Treat Sections~\ref{sec:A}--\ref{sec:opp} as the mechanism/derivations behind a research menu,
not as fixes to apply --- the verdict is the red box on page~1 and Section~\ref{sec:empirical}.

\section{[A] The 2p advantage in closed form, and its shrinkage}\label{sec:A}
\begin{varsheet}
$B_o$ & outcome magnitude in return units, $B_o=\cd{WIN\_BONUS}/\cd{PPO\_REWARD\_SCALE}$ ($=10$) \\
$w$ & number of \emph{wins} among the $G$ rollouts of a group \\
$\hat p$ & empirical group win-rate $w/G$ \\
$\tilde p$ & Beta-shrunk win-rate, Eq.~\eqref{eq:shrink} \\
$\alpha$ & \cd{GRPO\_ANALYTIC\_ALPHA}, the Beta pseudocount \\
$\Adv_{\mathrm{win}},\Adv_{\mathrm{loss}}$ & the two advantage values a 2p group can take \\
\end{varsheet}
In 2p the outcome is binary, so (ignoring draws and any decay) every $R_i\in\{+B_o,-B_o\}$. A group
with $w$ wins, $\hat p=w/G$, has \emph{exact} statistics
\begin{equation}
\mu_g=B_o(2\hat p-1),\qquad \sigma_g=2B_o\sqrt{\hat p(1-\hat p)},
\label{eq:binstats}
\end{equation}
and substituting into Eq.~\eqref{eq:grpo} (with $\varepsilon\!\to\!0$) the standardized advantage takes
\textbf{only two values}, a pure function of the win-rate:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\Adv_{\mathrm{win}}=\sqrt{\frac{1-\hat p}{\hat p}},\qquad
\Adv_{\mathrm{loss}}=-\sqrt{\frac{\hat p}{1-\hat p}}\;}
\label{eq:binadv}
\end{equation}
\emph{Derivation.} $\Adv_{\mathrm{win}}=\dfrac{B_o-\mu_g}{\sigma_g}=\dfrac{2B_o(1-\hat p)}{2B_o\sqrt{\hat p(1-\hat p)}}=\sqrt{\tfrac{1-\hat p}{\hat p}}$,
and symmetrically for the loss. Two consequences:
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.6em]
\item \textbf{Rare events are up-weighted.} As $\hat p\to0$ (a world you almost always lose) a rare win
  gets $\Adv_{\mathrm{win}}\to+\infty$ while the common losses get $\Adv_{\mathrm{loss}}\to0^-$: GRPO
  implicitly chases the rare win. This is a useful exploration prior --- when it is real.
\item \textbf{Every group carries unit gradient energy.} $\hat p\,\Adv_{\mathrm{win}}^2+(1-\hat p)\,\Adv_{\mathrm{loss}}^2=(1-\hat p)+\hat p=1$ for \emph{all} $\hat p$.
  A $99\%$ blowout pushes the gradient exactly as hard as a $50/50$ toss-up.
\end{itemize}
The collapse lives at $\hat p\!\in\!\{0,1\}$: then $\sigma_g=0$, the division falls back on
$\varepsilon$, and whatever infinitesimal residual distinguishes the (all-losing) rollouts --- a
length-dependent decay term, sampling noise --- is renormalised to magnitude $1$. The estimator
manufactures a confident gradient out of noise.

\paragraph{The fix: evaluate Eq.~\eqref{eq:binadv} at a shrunk $\tilde p$.} Replace the empirical
$\hat p$ by a Beta$(\alpha,\alpha)$ posterior mean,
\begin{equation}
\tilde p=\frac{w+\alpha}{G+2\alpha}\ \in(0,1),
\label{eq:shrink}
\end{equation}
and \emph{assign Eq.~\eqref{eq:binadv} by outcome class} (wins $\to\Adv_{\mathrm{win}}$, losses
$\to\Adv_{\mathrm{loss}}$, draws $\to0$). This (i) keeps $\tilde p$ strictly inside $(0,1)$, so there is
no $\varepsilon$-corner; (ii) gives \emph{every win in a group the same advantage and every loss the
same advantage}, so any within-class tiebreak (the stall signal) is gone by construction; and (iii) is
a \textbf{Rao--Blackwellisation} --- the outcome class is the sufficient statistic for a binary reward,
and conditioning on it replaces the noisy per-sample value with its exact conditional advantage, which
is variance-optimal among unbiased estimators that respect the outcome. In 4p the outcome is graded
(placement-linear), Eq.~\eqref{eq:binadv} no longer holds, and the code falls back to [B].

\section{[B] Drop the per-group standard deviation (and use leave-one-out)}\label{sec:B}
\begin{varsheet}
$b_g$ & a per-group baseline subtracted from $R_i$ \\
$\mu_{g,-i}$ & leave-one-out group mean, Eq.~\eqref{eq:loo} \\
$\mathcal E_g$ & per-group gradient \emph{energy} $\tfrac1G\sum_{i\in g}(\text{advantage})^2$ \\
$p(1-p)$ & the PFSP-``even'' matchmaking weight (\cd{rl\_math\_v9.tex}, league section) \\
\end{varsheet}
For a constant-per-rollout advantage the variance-minimising baseline is the expected return,
$b_g=\E[R\mid w_g]\approx\mu_g$; subtracting it is correct. \textbf{Dividing by $\sigma_g$ is not part
of any unbiased estimator} --- it is a per-group reweighting, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:binadv} showed exactly
what it does: it forces every group to unit energy. Compare the per-group energy with and without it:
\begin{equation}
\underbrace{\mathcal E_g^{\,/\sigma}=1}_{\text{current (DeepSeek)}}\quad\text{for all }\hat p;
\qquad
\underbrace{\mathcal E_g^{\,\text{center}}=\sigma_g^2=4B_o^2\,\hat p(1-\hat p)}_{\text{center-only (Dr.\ GRPO)}}.
\label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
The center-only energy is \textbf{peaked at $\hat p=\tfrac12$ and vanishes at the extremes} --- it
down-weights blowout/hopeless groups (nothing to learn) and concentrates the gradient on contested
ones. This is precisely the right curriculum, and it is \emph{the same function} $p(1-p)$ that
PFSP-``even'' uses to choose opponents. The league is already sampling matchups to maximise
$p(1-p)$; the $/\sigma_g$ term flattens that back out, so matchmaker and update were optimising against
each other. Removing it aligns them.

\paragraph{Implementation.} \cd{GRPO\_STD\_NORM=False} skips the division and applies \emph{one global
scale} to the batch (a single $\Std$ over all transitions), which --- unlike the per-group $\sigma_g$
--- preserves the cross-group $p(1-p)$ weighting while keeping the overall step size $O(1)$.

\paragraph{Leave-one-out baseline (RLOO).} \cd{GRPO\_LOO=True} replaces $\mu_g$ by the mean of the
\emph{other} $G-1$ rollouts,
\begin{equation}
\mu_{g,-i}=\frac1{G-1}\sum_{j\in g,\,j\neq i}R_j
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
R_i-\mu_{g,-i}=\frac{G}{G-1}\,(R_i-\mu_g).
\label{eq:loo}
\end{equation}
The baseline no longer contains sample $i$, removing the $O(1/G)$ self-correlation that biases the
included-mean estimator; the identity on the right shows the only effect on the \emph{magnitude} is a
benign $G/(G\!-\!1)$ rescale (here $16/15$), so the value of LOO is the de-biasing, not a scale change.

\section{[C] The shaping potential is a value, not a reward}\label{sec:C}
\begin{varsheet}
$\Phi(s)$ & shaping potential, $\Phi=\cd{SHAPE\_SHIP}\,\varphi_{\text{ship}}+\cd{SHAPE\_PROD}\,\varphi_{\text{prod}}$ \\
$\varphi_{\text{ship}},\varphi_{\text{prod}}$ & log ship-margin / production-share vs.\ the strongest opponent (Ng potentials) \\
$\gamma,\lambda$ & discount (\cd{GAMMA}) and GAE trace (\cd{GAE\_LAMBDA}) \\
$V_t,\widehat V_t$ & a value at $s_t$; the hand-built estimate Eq.~\eqref{eq:vhat} \\
$a$ & fitted slope of outcome on terminal position change, Eq.~\eqref{eq:phislope} \\
$\delta_t,\Adv_t$ & TD residual and the per-step (GAE) advantage \\
$L$ & rollout length; $\Delta\Phi_t=\Phi(s_t)-\Phi(s_0)$ \\
\end{varsheet}
GRPO's deepest weakness is that one trajectory-level scalar is smeared over all $T\!=\!500$ steps: no
\emph{per-step} credit. We already compute a hand-crafted ``who is winning'' signal $\Phi(s)$ and feed
it as potential-based reward shaping (PBRS). Under the Monte-Carlo return GRPO actually uses, that is
nearly wasted. Telescoping the shaping increment $\gamma\Phi_{t+1}-\Phi_t$ over a rollout,
\begin{equation}
\sum_{t=0}^{L-1}\big(\gamma\Phi_{t+1}-\Phi_t\big)
=\underbrace{-\,\Phi_0}_{\text{shared in group}}\;+\;(\gamma-1)\!\sum_{t=1}^{L-1}\!\Phi_t\;+\;\gamma\Phi_L .
\label{eq:telescope}
\end{equation}
The leading term $-\Phi_0$ is the world's \emph{initial} imbalance --- identical for all $G$ members of
a group (they share $w_g$, hence $s_0$) --- so the group mean $\mu_g$ \textbf{cancels it exactly}. What
survives is a tiny $(\gamma-1)=-0.003$-weighted occupancy integral, not the credit signal we wanted.
This is the textbook fact (Ng et al.; Wiewiora 2003) that \emph{PBRS is equivalent to initialising the
value function to $\Phi$, and only does work in the presence of bootstrapping.} With no critic there is
no bootstrapping, so the shaping is inert.

\paragraph{The fix: bootstrap with $\Phi$.} Use $\Phi$ as the value and run GAE. Anchor the value at the
group baseline and let position change move it:
\begin{equation}
\widehat V_t^{\,i}=\mu_{g(i)}+a\,\big(\Phi(s^i_t)-\Phi(s^i_0)\big)=\mu_{g}+a\,\Delta\Phi_t^{\,i},
\label{eq:vhat}
\end{equation}
with the slope fit once per iteration by closed-form least squares of the group-centred outcome on the
\emph{terminal} position change $\Delta\Phi_L$ (clamped to $[0,\cd{GRPO\_PHI\_A\_MAX}]$):
\begin{equation}
a=\frac{\sum_i (R_i-\mu_{g})\,\Delta\Phi^{\,i}_{L}}{\sum_i(\Delta\Phi^{\,i}_{L})^2}.
\label{eq:phislope}
\end{equation}
Then standard GAE on the (now sparse: terminal-outcome-only) reward $r_t$,
\begin{equation}
\delta_t=r_t+\gamma\widehat V_{t+1}-\widehat V_t,\qquad
\Adv_t=\sum_{l\ge0}(\gamma\lambda)^l\,\delta_{t+l}.
\end{equation}
Two things fall out. At the root $\widehat V_0=\mu_g$, so $\delta_0$ subtracts the group baseline:
\textbf{world difficulty is cancelled by the shared root}, recovering GRPO's whole purpose. And for an
interior step ($r_t=0$, $\gamma\approx1$),
\begin{equation}
\delta_t\approx a\big(\Phi(s_{t+1})-\Phi(s_t)\big),
\end{equation}
so the per-step advantage credits exactly the moves that \emph{improve your position}, with the
terminal outcome propagated back through them by the $(\gamma\lambda)$ trace. Because $\widehat V$ is a
function of state only, it is an \emph{unbiased baseline} regardless of fit quality (a bad fit costs
variance, never bias); the $\lambda<1$ trace is the usual bias--variance knob. To avoid double-counting
$\Phi$, the explicit reward shaping is turned off in this mode (\cd{GRPO\_PHI\_VALUE} routes $\Phi$ to
the value, and the dense reward is set to zero).

\section{[D] Common random numbers across a world-group}\label{sec:D}
\begin{varsheet}
$\xi_i,\zeta_i$ & the \emph{ego}'s and the \emph{opponent}'s action-sampling noise in rollout $i$ \\
$w_g$ & the (shared) world; comets are deterministic per world \\
$u,\,g_u$ & a uniform / Gumbel sample driving the categorical and the gate \\
$\Cov$ & covariance; the CRN variance identity below \\
\end{varsheet}
A rollout's return is a function of three independent noise sources,
$R_i=f(w_g,\xi_i,\zeta_i)$: the world (shared within a group), the ego's sampling $\xi_i$, and the
neural opponent's sampling $\zeta_i$. The group baseline is meant to isolate the effect of varying the
\emph{ego}'s actions, but with independent $\zeta_i$ it also absorbs opponent-sampling noise, inflating
$\sigma_g$ and loosening the advantage. \textbf{Common random numbers} couple the opponent across the
group: draw one noise stream $\zeta_g$ and share it among all $G$ members (the ego keeps independent
$\xi_i$ --- that variation is the signal). By the control-variate identity
\begin{equation}
\Var(R_i-R_j)=\Var R_i+\Var R_j-2\,\Cov(R_i,R_j),
\end{equation}
raising $\Cov$ between groupmates (via shared $\zeta$) lowers the variance of within-group differences,
hence of $\Adv_i$. Concretely the categorical \textbf{WHERE} is sampled by Gumbel-max with noise shared
across the group, and the \textbf{GATE} by a shared uniform:
\begin{equation}
d^i_s=\arg\max_{d}\big(\ell^i_{s,d}+g_{u}[g(i),s,d]\big),\qquad
f^i_s=\mathbb 1\!\big[u[g(i),s]<p^i_s\big],
\end{equation}
where $g_u,u$ are indexed by the \emph{group} $g(i)$, not the rollout --- identical within a group,
independent across groups. Identical-logit groupmates therefore sample identical actions (a clean unit
test); when logits differ (the ego has diverged the state) the actions differ but the \emph{noise} is
common, which is the variance reduction. CRN applies only to neural opponents (scripted seats are
handled in the engine); the world and its comets are already shared by construction.

\section{[C1] The rank advantage: bounded and reward-scale-free}\label{sec:rank}
\emph{\textbf{Verdict: this FAILED} --- the rank advantage collapsed the agent into passivity in the live
ablation (Section~\ref{sec:empirical}). The derivation below is the mechanism, not a recommendation.}
\begin{varsheet}
$r_i$ & within-group rank of $R_i$ (average rank on ties), $r_i\in[0,G-1]$ \\
$c_G$ & std of a uniform rank, $c_G=\sqrt{(G^2-1)/12}$ \\
\end{varsheet}
Section~\ref{sec:A} showed the std-normalised win advantage is $\Adv_{\mathrm{win}}=\sqrt{(1-\hat p)/\hat
p}$, which \textbf{diverges} as $\hat p\to0$. That is the precise engine of the second collapse: once the
snapshot pool is strong the learner sits in near-hopeless groups (small $\hat p$), and a single lucky win
is handed an arbitrarily large advantage, so the policy chases it and abandons the launches that lose
``honestly'' --- the passivity ratchet. [A]'s shrinkage caps the divergence at a finite
$\sqrt{(1-\tilde p)/\tilde p}$, but the worst case still grows with how lopsided the matchup is.

The \textbf{rank advantage} drops the dependence on $\hat p$ altogether: replace $R_i$ by its within-group
rank and standardise to unit variance,
\begin{equation}
\Adv_i=\frac{r_i-\tfrac{G-1}{2}}{c_G},\qquad c_G=\sqrt{\frac{G^2-1}{12}}
\label{eq:rank}
\end{equation}
(average rank for ties; $O(G^2)$ by pairwise comparison --- $G=16$, fully vectorised, no host sync). Two
properties:
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.6em]
\item \textbf{Bounded for every group.} The extreme rank gives
  $|\Adv_i|\le \dfrac{(G-1)/2}{c_G}=\sqrt{\dfrac{3(G-1)}{G+1}}\xrightarrow{G\to\infty}\sqrt3$ ($=1.63$ at
  $G=16$), \emph{independent of $\hat p$}. The lone win in a hopeless group gets $+1.63$, not $+\infty$.
\item \textbf{Invariant to the reward scale.} Eq.~\eqref{eq:rank} depends only on the \emph{ordering} of
  $\{R_i\}$, so it is unchanged by any monotone transform of the return --- \cd{PPO\_REWARD\_SCALE}, the
  \cd{WIN\_DECAY/LOSS\_DECAY} terms, every \cd{SHAPE\_*}/\cd{CAPTURE\_*} magnitude. The heavily hand-tuned
  reward constants stop steering the gradient.
\end{itemize}
This is the rank-shaping of natural evolution strategies (Wierstra et al.\ 2014): a small population's raw
fitness scale is unreliable, only its ordering is --- and the ordinal objective is what 4p placement
scores anyway. \textbf{Trade-off vs.\ [B]:} rank gives every group unit energy ($\mathcal E_g=1$, like the
$/\sigma_g$ path of Eq.~\eqref{eq:energy}), so it does \emph{not} restore the cross-group $p(1-p)$
weighting that center-only [B] does. Rank fixes the \emph{within-group} unboundedness; [B] fixes the
\emph{cross-group} difficulty inversion --- different axes. In code \cd{GRPO\_RANK\_ADV} short-circuits the
center-only path, so the trajectory estimator is rank \emph{or} Dr.\ GRPO+RMS (the two anti-collapse
bundles \cd{ANTICOLLAPSE} / \cd{DRGRPO\_BUNDLE} in \cd{grpo\_diag}).

\section{Tier 0: the trust region and the advantage scale}\label{sec:tier0}
Three cheap levers on the \emph{update} (not the advantage estimator) that compose with everything.

\paragraph{[D3] Clip-higher (\cd{CLIP\_HI}).} The symmetric clip $\clip(\rho,1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon)$
caps how fast a currently-unlikely action's probability can rise; with \cd{INIT\_GATE\_BIAS}$=-1$ (few
planets fire) that throttles exactly the launch exploration we need. DAPO's clip-higher relaxes only the
upper bound, $\clip(\rho,\,1-\varepsilon,\,1+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{hi}})$ with
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{hi}}=\cd{CLIP\_HI}>\varepsilon$, so positive-advantage probability-raising passes
while the downside stays tight. \cd{CLIP\_HI}$=0$ recovers the symmetric clip.

\paragraph{[D1] Length-normalised ratio (\cd{RATIO\_LENGTHNORM}).} The per-step log-ratio is a \emph{sum}
over owned planets, $\ell=\sum_{i\in\text{owned}}\Delta\log p_i$, so $\Var\ell\propto n_{\text{owned}}$ and
the fixed \cd{CLIP}/\cd{KL\_TARGET} bite hardest in many-planet (midgame) states. GSPO clips the
geometric-mean ratio $s=(\pol/\polold)^{1/n_{\text{owned}}}=\exp\!\big(\tfrac1{n_{\text{owned}}}\sum_i\Delta\log
p_i\big)$, making the trust region a function of policy change \emph{per decision}, not board size (it also
realigns the surrogate with the entropy term, already a per-owned-source mean). The KL early-stop reuses
the same normalised log-ratio.

\paragraph{[D4] Pinned advantage scale (\cd{ADV\_TARGET\_RMS}).} On the center-only path ([B],
\cd{GRPO\_STD\_NORM=False}) the single global scale is a \emph{batch} std that drifts with league
difficulty iteration-to-iteration. Since \cd{ADAM\_EPS}$=10^{-5}$ is not negligible against these small
sparse-reward gradients, that drift acts as a stealth learning-rate schedule keyed to which opponent was
drawn. \cd{ADV\_TARGET\_RMS}$>0$ rescales the centred advantage to a \emph{constant} RMS,
$\Adv\leftarrow(\Adv-\bar\Adv)\cdot\cd{ADV\_TARGET\_RMS}/\operatorname{rms}(\Adv)$, so the step size means
the same thing every iteration (one global scalar; still preserves the cross-group $p(1-p)$ weighting).

%================================================================
\section{[A1] Rao--Blackwellise the launch gate}\label{sec:rbgate}
\begin{varsheet}
$f_i,p_i$ & realised fire bit $\in\{0,1\}$ and its probability for owned planet $i$ \\
$\ell^{\mathrm w}_i$ & WHERE log-prob $\log p_{\text{where},i}(d_i)$ of the sampled destination \\
\end{varsheet}
The factored log-prob is $\log\pol=\sum_i[\log p_{\text{gate},i}(f_i)+f_i\,\ell^{\mathrm w}_i]$: the
destination score is gated by the \emph{sampled} bit $f_i$, so for the $1-p_i$ fraction of planets that did
not fire the WHERE gradient is identically zero this sample --- pure Monte-Carlo noise on the
strategically decisive head. Conditioning on the gate (Rao--Blackwell) replaces $f_i$ by its expectation,
$\E_{f_i\sim\text{Bern}(p_i)}[f_i\,\nabla\ell^{\mathrm w}_i]=p_i\,\nabla\ell^{\mathrm w}_i$, removing the
gate's sampling variance $p_i(1-p_i)\lVert\nabla\ell^{\mathrm w}_i\rVert^2$ \emph{exactly} (the gate is a
known Bernoulli --- no estimation). Implemented as a straight-through so the importance ratio stays honest:
the \emph{forward} value is the realised $f_i\ell^{\mathrm w}_i$ (the clip sees the true ratio) while the
\emph{gradient} flows as the de-noised $p_i\,\nabla\ell^{\mathrm w}_i$,
\begin{equation}
\widetilde{\ell^{\mathrm w}_i}=\big(f_i\,\ell^{\mathrm w}_i\big)^{\!\downarrow}+\bar p_i\,\ell^{\mathrm w}_i-\big(\bar p_i\,\ell^{\mathrm w}_i\big)^{\!\downarrow},\qquad (\cdot)^{\!\downarrow}=\text{stop-grad},\ \ \bar p_i=p_i^{\,\downarrow}.
\end{equation}
This is the discrete-sub-action marginalisation of expected policy gradients (Ciosek--Whiteson; ``mean
actor-critic''). The Tucker et al.\ (2018) ``mirage'' caveat is about \emph{learned} action-dependent
baselines, not the exact analytic marginalisation of a known Bernoulli, which is strictly
variance-reducing at zero extra forward cost. The gate keeps its own realised score (no double count).

%================================================================
\section{[C2] A risk-sensitive (CVaR) baseline}\label{sec:cvar}
The flat advantage optimises the group \emph{mean}, but the ladder is decided by not getting exploited by
the \emph{strong} opponents --- a mean-vs-tail mismatch (the recurring ``great training Elo, loses
head-to-head''). Optimise the worst-$\alpha$ tail: with the empirical $\alpha$-quantile $q_\alpha$ and
$\mathrm{CVaR}_\alpha=\tfrac1{\alpha G}\sum_{R_i\le q_\alpha}R_i$, baseline on the CVaR and up-weight the
tail,
\begin{equation}
\Adv_i=\big(R_i-\mathrm{CVaR}_\alpha\big)\,\big(1+\lambda\,\mathbb 1[R_i\le q_\alpha]\big),
\end{equation}
($\alpha=\cd{GRPO\_CVAR\_ALPHA}$, $\lambda=\cd{GRPO\_CVAR\_LAMBDA}$; \cd{kthvalue} on the $G$ axis, one
fused op). This is the CVaR policy gradient (Tamar; Chow); it pairs with [D] CRN, which makes the optimised
tail a pure function of \emph{ego} variance. \textbf{Risk:} over-pessimism re-summons the passivity basin
--- start mild ($\alpha\ge0.5$, $\lambda\approx1$) and watch \cd{lnch/st} against the tripwire.

%================================================================
\section{[B1] Per-step credit from shared-root siblings}\label{sec:sibling}
[C] manufactures per-step credit from a hand-built $\Phi$ with one global OLS slope. The $G$ rollouts of a
group are a richer, free source: they share $s_0$ and branch only on the ego's actions --- a tree rooted at
the world (the VinePPO setting, but the branches are already paid for). Bucket the siblings by a quantised
position signal at each step ($q_t=\lfloor\varphi_{\text{ship}}(s_t)/\Delta\rceil$,
$\Delta=\cd{GRPO\_SIBLING\_BUCKET\_DELTA}$) and use the matched-bucket mean \emph{terminal} return as a
per-step value, falling back to the whole-group mean when a bucket is thinner than
\cd{GRPO\_SIBLING\_MIN\_PEERS}:
\begin{equation}
\widehat V(s^i_t)=\!\!\operatorname*{mean}_{j:\,q_t(j)=q_t(i)}\!\!R_j,\qquad \Adv_t=\text{GAE}_{\gamma,\lambda}\big(r_t,\widehat V\big).
\end{equation}
Early steps (all siblings in one bucket) reduce to flat GRPO --- correct at the root; late steps separate
the siblings that pulled ahead from those that fell behind \emph{at matched position}, the per-step signal
the flat broadcast destroys. No critic, no extra rollout; composes with CRN (matched siblings then also
share opponent noise).

%================================================================
\section{[B2] The dead value head as a representation aux}\label{sec:aux}
Under GRPO the policy net's value head is never trained. \cd{GRPO\_AUX\_VALUE} trains it by MC regression to
the rollout return (PopArt-normalised), $\mathcal
L_{\mathrm{aux}}=\cd{GRPO\_AUX\_COEF}\cdot(V_\theta(s_t)-\text{PopArt}(R_i))^2$, \textbf{as an auxiliary
only} --- it never enters the advantage, so the update stays critic-free and the weights stay
interchangeable with a PPO checkpoint. The signal (``which states lead to winning'') regularises the shared
trunk toward outcome-relevant features; keep the coefficient small so it does not fight the policy. (This is
the safe cousin of the concurrent UNREAL \cd{AUX\_REWARD\_PRED} head, which regresses the immediate reward on
the PPO path.)

%================================================================
\section{[E1]/[E2] Opponent-conditioned baseline (one wired, one reserved)}\label{sec:opp}
Each iteration fills the whole batch with \emph{one} PFSP-sampled opponent, so a group's mean return depends
on which foe was drawn --- a between-iteration variance/bias source the world-baseline cannot see. The fix is
a control variate on opponent strength: subtract the league's own Elo-expected outcome before centering,
\begin{equation}
R_i\leftarrow R_i-\cd{GRPO\_OPP\_BASELINE\_W}\cdot(2\hat E_o-1)\,B_o,\qquad \hat E_o=\big(1+10^{(e_o-e_{\text{ego}})/s}\big)^{-1},
\end{equation}
so the advantage reads ``better than the rating system predicted vs.\ \emph{this} foe.'' \textbf{Caveat
(important).} With one opponent per iteration $\hat E_o$ is \emph{constant within every group}, so per-group
centering already removes it: \textbf{[E1] is a no-op on its own}. It only bites once groups \emph{span}
opponents --- which is \textbf{[E2] \cd{GRPO\_OPP\_STRATA}}: sample $K$ opponents per iteration, one per
contiguous block of groups, so the baseline averages the strength axis. [E2] needs a per-block opponent
rollout (each block evaluates a different opponent net), an invasive change to the rollout's per-step
opponent loop; it is \textbf{reserved, not wired} (Section~\ref{sec:tier3}). [E1]'s plumbing is in place as
its companion.

\section{The proof: a deterministic advantage-bound probe}\label{sec:proof}

\cd{analytic[A]} & $2.83$ & shrunk, but still grows \\

\section{Empirical result: the local ablation contradicts the theory}\label{sec:empirical}
\textbf{The Tier\,0--2 estimators above are NOT validated --- in a controlled GPU ablation they made things
\emph{worse}.} On an RTX 3070 Ti (\cd{scripts/\_gpu\_ab*.py}: h256 trunk, BC-warm shared init, identical
seed/worlds, 2p, 100 iters/arm) only \textbf{legacy per-group std-norm stayed stable}; every ``anti-collapse''
estimator drove the agent into the project's \emph{passivity basin} (launch-rate $\to 0$, Elo down):
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccl@{}}
\toprule
config & launch/st & Elo2 & verdict \\
\midrule
\textbf{legacy std-norm} (symmetric decay) & $0.70\!\to\!0.59$ & $1497\!\to\!\mathbf{1576}$ & \textbf{stable / improving} \\
rank[C1] $+$ clip-hi & $0.53\!\to\!0.10$ & $\to 1259$ & passivity \\
rank[C1] no-clip & $0.48\!\to\!0.27$ & $\to 1292$ & passivity \\
drgrpo[B] center-only & $0.57\!\to\!0.34$ & $\to 1370$ & passivity \\
drgrpo[B] $+$ clip-hi & $0.46\!\to\!0.03$ & $\to 1274$ & worst \\
sibling[B1] & $0.57\!\to\!0.24$ & $\to 1295$ & passivity ($|\Adv|_{\max}\!=\!16$) \\
phi-value[C] & $0.59\!\to\!0.31$ & $\to 1358$ & passivity ($|\Adv|_{\max}\!=\!22$) \\
\midrule
legacy $+$ ``lose-slower'' decay ($\cd{LOSS\_DECAY}<1$) & $0.55\!\to\!0.20$ & $\to 1305$ & \textbf{COLLAPSED} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Two conclusions, both confirmed:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \textbf{Bounded $\ne$ good.} The $\sqrt3$ bound (Section~\ref{sec:rank}) held \emph{exactly}, but the same
property that tames the lucky-win chase --- discarding win-\emph{magnitude} --- also removes the signal that
rewards \emph{decisive aggression}. The std rare-win amplification of Eq.~\eqref{eq:binadv} is double-edged: it
is the it426 spiral amplifier \emph{and} the thing that keeps the policy attacking. rank/drgrpo/sibling/phi-value
all weaken it and collapse into passivity; only std-norm keeps both.
\item \textbf{The it426 collapse was the reward TRIGGER, not the estimator.} The \emph{identical} legacy std-norm
is stable under symmetric decay and collapses under an asymmetric ``lose-slower'' decay (last row). So
Section~\ref{sec:setup}'s ``amplifier'' framing was half the story: removing the amplifier (Sections~\ref{sec:A},
\ref{sec:B}, \ref{sec:rank}) is the \emph{wrong} fix; \textbf{removing the trigger} (keep
$\cd{WIN\_DECAY}=\cd{LOSS\_DECAY}=1$) is the right one, and the v12 default already does.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Recommendation: train on legacy std-norm with symmetric decay} (the battle-tested v9/v12 default; the
\cd{setup1\_v12\_grpo\_a100.ipynb} preset is reverted to it). The estimator flags stay in the code for research
but must be re-ablated on the 3070 Ti before any A100 use; \cd{train.py} now prints the active estimator and
warns on the mutually-exclusive-flag footgun (the ``all flags on'' run that silently selected sibling/phi-value).
If the spiral ever recurs under legacy, the lever is \textbf{trust-region/entropy} (KL-to-reference
$\cd{GRPO\_KL\_COEF}>0$; an entropy floor), not an estimator swap.

\section{Pending: Tier 3 structural changes}\label{sec:tier3}
Designed but \emph{not implemented}; each is a larger surface than a flag flip. Listed by expected value.
\begin{description}[leftmargin=0pt,itemsep=2pt]
\item[\textnormal{\textbf{[E2] Opponent stratification.}}] The per-block multi-opponent rollout that makes
[E1] active: sample $K$ opponents, run each on its block of groups, update Elo per stratum. Cuts the
opponent-draw variance $\sim1/K$ and gives $K\times$ cleaner ratings. Touches the rollout opponent loop and
the Elo update.
\item[\textnormal{\textbf{[E3] Seat-antithetic mirror pairing.}}] Within a group, pair rollouts that
\emph{swap} which seat the ego plays (the deterministic seat-swap tiling already exists). In a near-zero-sum
game $O(\text{ego}{=}0)\approx-O(\text{ego}{=}1)$, so the pair-sum cancels world \emph{and} absolute-seat
advantage --- an antithetic control variate that also kills the documented seat-0 positional overfit. 2p
first (the 4p swap is a 4-cycle, not an involution).
\item[\textnormal{\textbf{[A2] Antithetic ego coupling.}}] Extend CRN [D] to the ego: antithetic gate
Bernoullis ($U$ and $1-U$) across paired groupmates give negatively-correlated returns, lowering the
variance of the $G$-sample baseline for free (throughput, not compute, is the binding constraint).
\item[\textnormal{\textbf{[D2] Per-factor clipping.}}] Clip each planet's $\Delta\log p_i$ then sum, instead
of clipping the joint product ratio --- the token-level end of the GSPO/PPO spectrum at the correct
(per-planet) granularity, so one bad planet can neither escape nor veto the clip. Needs a per-source
log-prob from the distributions.
\item[\textnormal{\textbf{[D5] Adaptive KL-to-reference.}}] Replace the bang-bang KL early-stop with a smooth
penalty whose $\beta$ is driven by a PI controller to a KL setpoint (the $k3$ machinery is already present);
a better-conditioned trust region that makes more update epochs safe.
\item[\textnormal{\textbf{[C3]/[E4] Calibrated / Elo-graded outcome.}}] Replace $\sign(\cdot)$ by a
saturating $\tanh$ ship-margin (training-time densification of the one informative scalar) and/or grade the
outcome by the Elo residual so beating a strong foe counts more. Saturation $+$ decay guard against
score-running; report only the binary outcome.
\item[\textnormal{\textbf{[B3] Per-factor counterfactual credit (COMA).}}] Treat each owned planet as an
agent; give its gate/WHERE a counterfactual advantage from sibling launch-event differencing (``which launch
won it''). Builds on [B1]'s sibling buckets.
\item[\textnormal{\textbf{[B4] Hindsight credit assignment.}}] Return-conditioned HCA reweighting
$(1-\pol/h)\,R$ with a small hindsight head $h(a\mid s,R)$; the factored action makes $h$ cheap. The
principled version of [B1].
\item[\textnormal{\textbf{[E5] Worst-case / PSRO weighting.}}] Weight each iteration's advantage by
$(1-\hat E_o)^\beta$ (focus on foes you cannot yet beat) $\to$ a min-exploitability objective, the
principled answer to the non-transitive cycling that average-league-return induces. The full version solves a
Nash meta-distribution over the league payoff matrix (PSRO).
\end{description}
\noindent Every Tier 3 item must be judged on \textbf{held-out head-to-head}, never training Elo --- the
project's standing lesson (a high-Elo agent that finishes last in H2H is the failure these all target).
